# Top 5 Strongest Hindu Gods — Treatment

Woodcut Print · 1080×1920 vertical · 24 fps · 77.95 s · English · narration: the user's ElevenLabs take 1 (Jarnathan), used as is.

## Three candidate structures

1. **Five plates, one block recut.** One wood block carved deeper for each god; what was cut for Hanuman is cut away for Indra. Strong idea, but every god loses their own plate, and a ranking wants five distinct prints.
2. **A temple-door pull-back.** One continuous pull-back across a carved temple door, each god a panel. Elegant, but at phone size the panels get tiny and the ranking stops reading as a countdown.
3. **A countdown of five prints, the colour rising.** Each god is a self-contained plate (the wordless-novel grammar). Each opens on its rank numeral carved *mirrored* in the block; the paper is pulled and the numeral reads true. The one colour, a saffron-vermilion (sindoor) plate, stands for divine power: a single glowing herb for Hanuman, a thunderbolt for Indra, a lion's mane and weapon tips for Durga, the cosmos under Vishnu's stride, and finally Shiva's whole ring of fire. **Chosen**: a countdown needs the climb, and the colour climbing with the rank is the film's one native move made into structure.

## Logline and arc

Five gods, five prints: as the rank rises, the fire carved into the block grows from one herb to the end of the universe.

- **Setup** (0–5.6 s): an uncut black block. A single knife line carves an eye-shaped flame; the title is carved, mirrored, then pulled true.
- **Turn** (5.6–58.8 s): four plates, each bigger in scale than the last: a mountain lifted, rivers freed, a demon ended, the cosmos crossed in three steps.
- **Ending** (58.8–77.95 s): Shiva's third eye opens and the colour floods the whole cut, then his ring of fire. On the closing question, the five prints lie stacked as a fan of sheets, numerals showing: the audience picks.

## Benchmark

- **19th-century Battala woodcuts of Kolkata** (popular religious woodblock prints): learn the frontal, iconic staging of a deity, the decorative frame, and how few cuts a face needs. Don't copy: any specific print, its border designs or figures.
- **Frans Masereel, *Passionate Journey*** (1919): learn the self-contained plates, the reading time a locked plate gets, light radiating from one source. Don't copy: plates, figures, compositions.
